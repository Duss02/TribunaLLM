The Basic Income Earth Network defines a basic income as a periodic cash payment unconditionally delivered to all on an individual basis, without a means test or work requirement. It lists five features: periodic, cash, individual, universal and unconditional.

Since 1982 all Alaska residents have received a yearly cash dividend from the Alaska Permanent Fund. The Alaska Department of Revenue announced in September 2025 that the 2025 dividend, set by House Bill 53, was $1,000 for each of more than 600,000 eligible Alaskans.

Damon Jones and Ioana Marinescu used Current Population Survey data and a synthetic control method and reported in the American Economic Journal: Economic Policy in 2022 that the Alaska dividend had no effect on employment and increased part-time work by 1.8 percentage points, or 17 percent.

In a GiveDirectly experiment in rural Kenya, adults in some villages receive about $22.50 per month, roughly $0.75 per day, for 12 years, while others receive it for 2 years or a one-time $500 lump sum. Researchers led by Tavneet Suri reported in December 2023 that total labor supply did not change, with hours shifting from wage work to self-employment.

The Kenya results published in December 2023 cover only the first two years of a 12-year design. The researchers, including Abhijit Banerjee and Paul Niehaus, plan further surveys at 7 and 12 years to see whether long-term payments eventually outperform the lump sum, and the sample is drawn from rural Kenyan villages.

In Germany's Pilotprojekt Grundeinkommen, 122 volunteers received 1,200 euros per month for three years, from June 2021 to May 2024. DIW Berlin reported in April 2025, comparing 107 recipients with 1,580 controls, that recipients neither withdrew from the labor market nor significantly reduced their working hours.

Finland's basic income experiment gave 2,000 unemployed people 560 euros per month tax-free in 2017 and 2018. Kela reported in 2020 that from November 2017 to October 2018 recipients worked 78 days on average, 6 days more than the control group, and reported better well-being, but said an activation model introduced in 2018 complicated interpretation.

In Stockton, California, 125 randomly selected residents of neighborhoods at or below the city median income received $500 per month for 24 months from February 2019. Researchers Stacia West and Amy Castro Baker reported in March 2021 that recipients' full-time employment rose from 28 percent to 40 percent after one year, while the control group's rate rose by 5 percentage points.

Evelyn Forget reported in Canadian Public Policy in 2011, on Manitoba's MINCOME guaranteed annual income experiment of 1974 to 1979, an 8.5 percent reduction in the hospitalization rate for participants relative to controls, mainly for accidents, injuries and mental health. She also found that more adolescents continued into grade 12.

In February 2026 the Irish cabinet approved a permanent Basic Income for the Arts scheme paying 325 euros per week to 2,000 artists in three-year cycles, with 18.27 million euros allocated in Budget 2026, RTE reported. RTE also reported that a cost-benefit analysis of the pilot, which began in September 2022, found a return of 1.39 euros for every euro invested.

Dennis Egger and colleagues reported in Econometrica in 2022 that one-time transfers of about $1,000 to over 10,500 poor households across 653 randomized villages in rural Kenya produced minimal price inflation, positive spillovers to non-recipient households and firms, and a local transfer multiplier of 2.5.

In a randomized study by Eva Vivalt and colleagues at OpenResearch, 1,000 low-income adults in Illinois and Texas received $1,000 per month for three years and 2,000 controls received $50 per month. An NBER working paper issued in July 2024 and revised in August 2026 reports that recipients' income excluding the transfers fell by about $1,900 per year, labor market participation fell by 4.2 percentage points, and work hours fell by 1 to 2 hours per week.

The same NBER working paper by Vivalt and colleagues found no impact on the quality of employment, and found that subjective well-being was higher among recipients in the first year before reverting to control group levels. According to OpenResearch, participants were aged 21 to 40 with household incomes below 300 percent of the federal poverty line, so the sample was not drawn from all adults.

The Tax Foundation estimated in July 2019 that Andrew Yang's proposed Freedom Dividend of $1,000 per month for about 236 million adult citizens would cost $2.8 trillion per year. It estimated that a 10 percent value-added tax with a very broad base would raise about $952 billion per year.

Modeling studies of a US basic income reach opposite results. The Penn Wharton Budget Model estimated in March 2018 that deficit-financed payments of $500 per month to every adult would reduce GDP by 6.1 percent by 2027. A 2017 Roosevelt Institute model of $1,000 per month estimated GDP would rise 12.56 percent over eight years if debt-financed, and Fortune reported that it did not examine effects on job-seeking.

Economists Hilary Hoynes and Jesse Rothstein wrote in the Annual Review of Economics in 2019 that, compared with existing US programs, a UBI would direct much larger shares of transfers to childless, nonelderly, nondisabled households and much more to middle-income than poor households. They wrote that a UBI large enough to increase transfers to low-income families would be enormously expensive.

Most pilots tested temporary or targeted payments rather than a permanent payment to every adult. Finland paid 2,000 unemployed people, the German pilot enrolled volunteers aged 21 to 40 in single-person households, and the OpenResearch study drew low-income adults. Hoynes and Rothstein argued in 2019 that ongoing UBI pilots would do little to resolve the major outstanding questions.

On 5 June 2016 Swiss voters rejected the popular initiative for an unconditional basic income, with 76.9 percent voting no. The initiative specified neither the amount nor the financing, according to the Swiss government; campaigners had suggested 2,500 francs per month for adults and 625 francs for children.

A Pew Research Center survey of 11,001 US adults conducted from 27 July to 2 August 2020 found 54 percent opposed and 45 percent favored the federal government providing a guaranteed income of about $1,000 per month for all adult citizens, whether or not they work.

Ontario's Basic Income Pilot, launched in 2017 for three years, enrolled about 4,000 low-income people in Hamilton, Brantford, Thunder Bay and Lindsay, paying single people up to 16,989 Canadian dollars per year, reduced by 50 percent of earnings. The province cancelled it in 2018, and Social Services Minister Lisa MacLeod called it a disincentive to get people back on track, Global News reported.

As of May 2024, Arkansas, Idaho, Iowa and South Dakota had passed laws prohibiting guaranteed income programs, NPR reported. In April 2024 the Texas Supreme Court temporarily blocked a Harris County program that planned $500 payments to about 1,900 randomly selected residents.
