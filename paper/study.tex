\section{Study 2: where judges err together}
\label{sec:study2}

The correlated-error result of \S\ref{sec:llm} invites a question it cannot answer: is the weaker overlap between \jev{} and an LLM a property of the kind of judge, or of a task whose labels are free and whose evidence is fixed? \citet{rao2026jev} measured overlap on subjective rubric panels with human labels; I measured it on construction-labelled contradiction pairs. The two differ in task, evidence, judges and question at once. Study 2 separates them. It was pre-registered before any new API call (hypotheses, items, judges, measures, stopping rule; the pre-registration and its deviations ship with the code), and every result is reported whether or not it matches the prediction.

\subsection{Hypotheses}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.6em]
\item[H1] \emph{Task objectivity.} For the same pair of judges, the correlation of errors ($\phi$ between the two error indicators) is lower on \dc{} than on a subjective panel with human labels. Support: lower for at least 80\% of pairs, intervals disjoint for at least half.
\item[H2] \emph{Kind of judge.} On \dc{}, with identical evidence, error correlation is ordered: two LLMs $>$ a decision model with an LLM $>$ two decision models of different makers (\jev{} and GPT-6 Luna through OpenAI's Decisions API). Stated before any Luna Decisions data existed.
\item[H3] \emph{Error type.} \jev{}'s recall on altered entities, attributions, polarities and units is lower than on altered numbers by at least 15 points; GPT-6 Luna's falls by less than 10.
\item[H4] \emph{Difficulty.} Within strata defined by whether a third judge was right, \jev{}--LLM correlation stays below LLM--LLM correlation.
\item[H5] \emph{Framing replicates.} The framed criterion's AUROC gain over the relation question (for \jev{}, \S\ref{sec:framing}) replicates for GPT-6 Luna Decisions, with a gain of at least 0.05.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Items, judges and measures}

\paragraph{Items.} The \nPairs{} numeric pairs of \S\ref{sec:dataset} are unchanged. To them I add 1,657 pairs of four new types, built from the same supported sentences with the same rule (the original token occurs in the evidence the judge reads, the replacement occurs nowhere in it, one edit per sentence, seed 2026): \emph{polarity} (a direction word flipped: rose/fell, more/fewer, higher/lower; 269 pairs), \emph{entity} (a country or city replaced by one of the same kind absent from the evidence; 741), \emph{attribution} (the source named for a fact replaced by another of the same kind; 305) and \emph{unit} (percent/percentage points, million/billion, per year/per month, dollars/euros; 342). Direction words are too common for the whole-evidence rule, so for polarity the rule is applied to the passages that state the sentence's fact; this amendment was recorded before any judge ran. The split by motion is unchanged (held-out: crewed spaceflight, rent control; 407 numeric and 400 new-type pairs). Every candidate edit is checked by code and rejections are logged.

\paragraph{Judges.} Every judge answers the same framed criterion (\S\ref{sec:framing}) over the same evidence text, in two conditions: the twenty passages the arena read and the three that best match the sentence by BM25. Six judges of three kinds: two decision models of different makers, \jev{} 1.13.0 and GPT-6 Luna through the Decisions API (a predicate question, probability returned, input tokens only billed); three LLM judges answering with a JSON verdict at reasoning effort \emph{low}, GPT-6 Luna, DeepSeek V4 Pro and Claude Sonnet 5.5 (the last on a seeded sample of 400 held-out pairs, 200 numeric and 50 per new type; DeepSeek on every numeric pair and 1,262 of the 1,657 new-type pairs, where its balance ran out); and an NLI consistency classifier, Vectara's HHEM-2.1-Open, run on the CPU with the three passages as premise. Decision models and HHEM decide at 0.5, with a threshold fitted on the training motions reported beside it; an LLM answer without a parsable verdict is an abstention, excluded and counted in coverage.

\paragraph{Subjective panels.} For H1 the same judges grade two rubric panels with human labels through the Autorubric library: CHARM-100 (the library's own example, 100 conversations, six criteria; primary) and HealthBench-100 test (100 items with physician majority labels per criterion; secondary), both obtained before any judge ran on them. Ordinal criteria are read as MET in the upper half of their scale; the nominal length criterion and not-applicable labels are not used. \jev{} runs under the library's \texttt{noul\_framed} framing, the LLM judges through LiteLLM at thinking level \emph{low}, Luna Decisions with one typed question per criterion. HHEM answers no question on these panels (no criterion compares the submission with a source), DeepSeek's API rejected the library's structured-output format on the day of the run, and Claude was stopped after CHARM-100 by the pre-registered spending rule; the pre-registration lists these deviations.

\paragraph{Measures.} Per judge: accuracy, recall on altered sentences, false-alarm rate on unchanged ones, AUROC where a probability exists, overall and by type. Per pair of judges, on the items both answered: the $2\times2$ table of error indicators, $\phi$, Cohen's $\kappa$, $P(B\ \text{wrong} \mid A\ \text{wrong})$ against $P(B\ \text{wrong})$, and the effective number of independent votes $k/(1+(k-1)\bar\phi)$, the design-effect correction for equicorrelated units \citep{kish1965survey}. Coverage at guaranteed risk: thresholds chosen on the training motions as the widest acceptance region whose one-sided Clopper--Pearson 95\% bound on the error is at most 5\%, evaluated on the held-out motions. Intervals are percentile bootstraps resampling pairs (conversations on the panels), 1,000 resamples. Thresholds, selective rules and nothing else are fitted on the training motions; the held-out motions were not opened until every judge had finished on them.

\paragraph{Exploratory: shared evidence, shared errors.} This check was not pre-registered. Three passages chosen by BM25 do not always contain the fact: the passage carrying the original token is among them for \studyRetrievalShare{} of held-out pairs. Table~\ref{tab:study-retrieval} splits recall by this. Where the fact passage is present, the LLM judges catch most altered sentences (Luna chat \studyRetrRecallPresentLunaChat{}, DeepSeek \studyRetrRecallPresentDeepseekPro{}, Claude \studyRetrRecallPresentClaude{}); where it is absent they fall to \studyRetrRecallAbsentLunaChat{}, \studyRetrRecallAbsentDeepseekPro{} and \studyRetrRecallAbsentClaude{}, and the correlation between two LLMs' errors is \studyRetrPhiAbsentLunaChatDeepseekPro{} in that stratum against \studyRetrPhiPresentLunaChatDeepseekPro{} where the fact is present (Table~\ref{tab:study-retrieval-corr}). \jev{} over three passages falls from \studyRetrRecallPresentJevThree{} to \studyRetrRecallAbsentJevThree{}, and over twenty, where the fact passage is always read, stays at \studyRetrRecallAbsentJevTwoZero{} on the same pairs. Much of what the LLM judges share on \dc{} is therefore the evidence they were handed: models that read the three passages carefully and find no passage about the fact all answer the same way, and that answer is wrong by construction. Shared error has an evidence component that a judge-only account misses, and that the twenty-passage condition removes.

\subsection{The arena with the certified question}
\label{sec:arena-v2}

Study 2 certifies one question for the arena's retraction: the framed criterion over every passage the arena read, with \jev{} deciding at 0.5 or, for a strike that has to be right, at the confident threshold of the guaranteed-risk rule ($p \geq \studyLbThreshold{}$, about 5\% error on held-out \dc{}). I judged every claim of the \studyLbDebates{} pilot debates again under it: \studyLbClaims{} claims, \studyLbCost{} of judge time, no new debate, because every sentence is stored with the passages it was judged against. The pilot's own question had struck \studyLbPilotStruck{} claims. The framed criterion marks \studyLbContradicted{} at 0.5 (\studyLbRateAll{}) and \studyLbContradictedConfident{} at the confident rule (\studyLbRateAllConfident{}).

The ends of the ranking are separated, the neighbours are not. At the 0.5 rule the two DeepSeek models stand apart from the other four (\studyLbRateDeepseekVFourPro{} and \studyLbRateDeepseekVFourFlash{} of their claims against \studyLbRateGptSixOneSol{} to \studyLbRateClaudeOpusFiveFive{}), with disjoint intervals, and the four overlap among themselves. The spread the leaderboard was built to show exists, and it broadly follows the pilot's match outcomes at the top (the two Claude models and GPT-6.1 Sol) and the bottom (GPT-6 Luna). Two cautions belong with it. The rate varies more across motions than across models (Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard-v2-packs}): motions whose packs are dense with figures (sugar tax, nuclear power) produce more strikes for everyone, so the comparison holds within a schedule in which every model argued every motion on both sides, as here, and not across schedules. And a strike means that the library gives a different figure for the same thing, which the pilot's hand review showed can be an edition conflict (Lazard's 2023 against 2024 cost ranges, a study quoted from a later revision) rather than a falsehood; the \studyLbContradictedConfident{} confident strikes are listed with the data for review, and the arena still needs a third verdict, ``not in this edition'', before a strike can be read as an error of the debater rather than a disagreement with the library.

\subsection{What the study says}

Of five pre-registered predictions, two held (H2, H3), one held in part (H4) and two failed (H1, H5); the failed predictions carry the most information. Error overlap between judges is ordered by the kind of judge: two reasoning models of different families are worth \studyVotesLunaChatDeepSeekPro{} votes, a decision model and an LLM more, two decision models of different makers most, and an NLI classifier is independent of all of them but too weak to use. The overlap is not lower on a task with free labels in general; it is lower for pairs that include a decision model and higher for pairs of LLMs, and the exploratory split shows why: LLMs that read the same three passages fail together when the fact is not in them, so part of what looks like shared judgement is shared evidence. The framing that moved \jev{} from catching one altered number in twenty to nine in ten moves a second decision model the other way, so the lesson of \S\ref{sec:framing} is not that the framed criterion should be adopted everywhere, but that the question has to be tuned to the judge that answers it, with labels one can afford. \jev{}'s weakness has a shape, units and attributions, where an LLM second opinion is worth having and where the human check of the construction labels is due. And \jev{} over twenty passages remains the best single judge on this task and the only one whose probability supports a guaranteed-risk rule, while agreeing with human labels on the subjective panels as well as the LLMs do, the result the Autorubric authors reported, reproduced here on their material with their library. With that question the arena does what it was built for: the same 180 debates, judged again for \studyLbCost{}, give \studyLbContradictedConfident{} confident strikes instead of \studyLbPilotStruck{} and a leaderboard whose ends are separated.
