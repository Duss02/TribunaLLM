Six Apollo missions landed on the Moon between 1969 and 1972, and twelve astronauts walked on its surface. The Planetary Society, using NASA budget documents, puts Apollo spending at 25.8 billion dollars in nominal terms for 1960 to 1973, about 309 billion dollars in 2025 dollars.

Artemis II launched on 1 April 2026 with four crew on a lunar flyby lasting 9 days, 1 hour and 32 minutes. NASA says the crew traveled about 252,756 miles, a record for the greatest distance humans have traveled in space. NASA lists Artemis III for 2027 as a docking test in low Earth orbit and the first landing on Artemis IV in 2028.

The International Space Station has had continuous human presence since November 2000. NASA's Inspector General reported in November 2019 that NASA spends between 3 billion and 4 billion dollars a year to operate the station, including payments for crew and cargo transportation.

Between 1969 and 1972 six Apollo missions brought back 382 kilograms of lunar material, about 2,200 separate samples from six sites, according to NASA's lunar sample curators. Three automated Soviet Luna spacecraft returned 300 grams from three other sites.

On Apollo 17 in December 1972 the crew drove 35.7 kilometers in the lunar rover during three moonwalks totaling about 22 hours. The crew included geologist Harrison Schmitt, whom NASA calls the first scientist on the Moon. NASA's Lunar Sample Compendium describes the orange soil from Shorty crater as mostly volcanic glass about 3.6 billion years old.

Hubble was launched on 24 April 1990 with a flawed primary mirror, and astronauts serviced it on five Space Shuttle missions between December 1993 and May 2009. The first mission installed corrective optics. Unlike Hubble, the James Webb Space Telescope does not orbit Earth; NASA places it about 1.5 million kilometers away at the L2 point.

In four spacewalks between 15 November 2019 and 25 January 2020, astronauts Luca Parmitano and Andrew Morgan replaced the cooling system of the Alpha Magnetic Spectrometer, a cosmic-ray detector on the International Space Station that was not designed to be serviced in orbit.

After an oxygen tank explosion damaged Apollo 13 about 200,000 miles from Earth in April 1970, the crew and ground team improvised a carbon dioxide scrubber adapter, and the three astronauts returned safely on 17 April 1970.

NASA's Twins Study compared astronaut Scott Kelly, during about a year on the International Space Station, with his identical twin Mark on Earth. NASA reported that 93 percent of Scott's genes returned to normal after landing, while the other 7 percent pointed to possible longer-term changes in immune function, DNA repair and bone formation.

The NASA Twins Study, published in Science in 2019, examined one astronaut during a year-long mission, with his earthbound twin as the genetic control. It reported spaceflight-specific changes including decreased body mass, telomere elongation and genome instability, and said that expression of some genes was still disrupted after return.

NASA says the International Space Station hosted nearly 3,000 research investigations through Expedition 60 from researchers in more than 108 countries.

NASA's rover Opportunity, designed to last 90 Martian days, landed on 24 January 2004 and drove 45.16 kilometers before contact was lost on 10 June 2018. Its twin Spirit became stuck in soft sand on 1 May 2009 and last communicated on 22 March 2010.

China's robotic Chang'e 5 returned 1,731 grams of lunar samples in December 2020. Chang'e 6 landed on the far side on 1 June 2024, and Xinhua reported that it returned 1,935.3 grams of samples when it came back on 25 June 2024.

NASA says Voyager 1 and Voyager 2, both launched in 1977, reached the interstellar boundary in 2012 and 2018 respectively, and both remain operational.

NASA planned to spend about 2.75 billion dollars on the Perseverance rover over 11 years: 2.2 billion for hardware, 243 million for launch and 291 million for 2.5 years of operations. The Mars Exploration Rover mission cost 820 million dollars for the 90-day primary mission of two rovers.

NASA's Inspector General projected in November 2021 that NASA would spend 93 billion dollars on the Artemis effort from fiscal 2012 through fiscal 2025. It estimated the production and operating cost of one Space Launch System and Orion launch at 4.1 billion dollars for Artemis I through IV, excluding prior development.

NASA's Inspector General reported on 1 May 2024 that NASA would have spent more than 55 billion dollars on the Space Launch System, Orion and ground systems by September 2025, then the planned date for Artemis II. It judged that Orion heat shield material wearing away at more than 100 locations on Artemis I posed a significant risk to crew safety.

Fourteen astronauts died in two Space Shuttle accidents, Challenger on 28 January 1986 and Columbia on 1 February 2003, in a program of 135 missions. Three astronauts died in the Apollo 1 cabin fire on 27 January 1967.

Radiation measurements aboard Curiosity during a 253-day, 560-million-kilometer flight to Mars led Zeitlin and colleagues (Science, 2013) to calculate a dose equivalent of 0.66 plus or minus 0.12 sievert for even the shortest round trip with current propulsion systems and comparable shielding.

NASA's Inspector General wrote in November 2021 that Artemis aimed to return humans to the Moon by late 2024, and projected that NASA would exceed that timetable by several years. NASA's current Artemis plan targets the first landing on Artemis IV in 2028.

Crewed and robotic cost figures are not like for like. The NASA Inspector General's 4.1 billion dollar per-launch figure for Artemis excludes prior development, the 2.75 billion dollar Perseverance figure covers development, launch and 2.5 years of operations, and the 93 billion dollar Artemis total is a projection made in 2021.
